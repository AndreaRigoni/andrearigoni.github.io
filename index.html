Research

Machine learning for diagnostic data integration

A fusion experiment is observed by thousands of heterogeneous sensors: RFX-mod alone had 8,336 signal nodes (magnetics, temperature, density, wall interaction) and acquired about 10 GB in a 250 ms discharge. The physics linking those signals is non-linear and largely hidden. My Ph.D. thesis, “Tools for heterogeneous diagnostic data integration: application to real-time control of fusion relevant plasmas” (University of Padova, 2021), proposed learning that link directly from data with deep generative models, and running the result fast enough to be used for control.

Integrate

A shared latent state from many diagnostics

Variational autoencoders trained on each diagnostic are composed into one model whose latent space describes the plasma state as seen by all of them. The composition acts as a structural sparse regulariser and makes diagnostic-to-diagnostic mapping possible, for example inferring Soft X-ray temperature profiles from the magnetic configuration.

Recover

Reconstruction, denoising, compression

The same latent representation recovers missing or faulty channels, reduces noise, flags anomalies and compresses high-rate data. On RFX-mod Soft X-ray data, VAE reconstruction of missing points beats B-spline interpolation, and learned codecs are being studied for real-time data compression.

Deploy

Real-time inference on FPGA

Models are quantized (quantization-aware training, Brevitas, FINN, hls4ml, HGQ) and synthesised into FPGA firmware so they run inside the acquisition chain. A neural surrogate of the RFX-mod2 toroidal equilibrium runs a full radial scan in about 300 ns, far inside the 100 µs control cycle.

RFX-hunch — a distributed, unsupervised plasma-state control. The Ph.D. idea became a research line at Consorzio RFX that I lead: sensor data are aggregated into a learned state, physics models are probed against it, and ML controllers act on it alongside the legacy RFX control system. Each block is now a student thesis, from data compression to VAE reconstruction to toroidal magnetic reconstruction (see Mentorship).

Data acquisition & analysis · Teaching

MDSplus and the Concurrent and Real-Time Programming course at UniPD

My work on MDSplus and my course at the University of Padova are two sides of the same subject. MDSplus is where concurrency, real-time constraints and distributed data access meet a real experiment; the course is where I teach those same techniques to the next generation of engineers, many of whom then join acquisition and data projects at Consorzio RFX.

Since 2015 · Consorzio RFX · with MIT PSFC

MDSplus core developer

MDSplus is a framework for the acquisition, storage and access of complex scientific data, developed jointly by MIT and the fusion research group in Padova. It is the most widely used data management system in the magnetic fusion energy programme, installed at over 30 sites on 4 continents.

  • Data throughput over high-latency links (Padova ↔ ITER Remote Experimentation Centre, Rokkasho, JP)
  • Code quality, test-driven development and contribution workflow
  • Portable build system (Autoconf/Automake) and containerisation
  • Custom FPGA acquisition devices (Zynq, RedPitaya) streaming into MDSplus for RFX-mod2, SPIDER and MITICA

Worked closely with Tom Fredian and Josh Stillerman (MIT PSFC), Keith Erickson (PPPL) and Gabriele Manduchi (RFX).
